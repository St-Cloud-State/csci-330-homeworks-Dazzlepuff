Our rewritten grammar rules:

I 🡺 iES | iESeS	
E 🡺 GE'
E' 🡺 oGE' | ε	
G 🡺 x|y|z|w	 
S 🡺 s | dLb	
L 🡺 s | sL


Function for I (Starting Rule)
    If the lookahead is i, we choose between:
        I → i E S e S
        I → i E S e S e S
    Since both productions start with i, we match i and call E, followed by S.
    If e appears next, we know it's the first production.
    If another S e follows, we use the second production.

Function for E
    If the lookahead is G (x, y, z, w):
        We match G and call E'.

Function for E'
    If the lookahead is o, we match o, then call G, and then call E' recursively.
    If the lookahead is anything else, we return (ε-production).

Function for G
    If the lookahead is x, y, z, or w, we match and return.

Function for S
    If the lookahead is s, we match s (corresponding to S → s).
    If the lookahead is d, we match d, then call L, and finally match b (corresponding to S → d L b).
    Since s also appears in L, further lookahead is needed to determine if we are in S → s or S → d L b.

Function for L
    If the lookahead is s, we match s and:
        If another s follows, we continue calling L recursively (L → s L).
        Otherwise, we stop (L → s).

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

i:
• The only rule that begins with i is for I (both alternatives of I start with i).
• When the lookahead is i, the parser knows to call the function for I.

e:
• The terminal e appears only in the production I → i E S e S.
• When e is encountered (right after the first S in I), it signals that the longer production is being used.

o:
• The only appearance of o is in E′ (i.e., E′ → o G E′).
• If the lookahead is o while processing E′, the parser matches o and continues with G E′.

x:
• x is one of the four terminals produced by G (the others being y, z, and w).
• Seeing x (while expecting a G) uniquely determines that G should produce x.

y:
• Similar to x, y is produced by G.
• When the lookahead is y in the context of G, the parser chooses G → y.

z:
• z is also produced by G.
• Its appearance tells the parser that G should yield z.

w:
• w is the remaining terminal in G.
• Encountering w means G → w is the correct production.

s:
• The letter s appears in two places: as a complete production S → s and as the beginning of L in S → d L b (with L → s or L → s L).
• When the lookahead is s, additional context (or further lookahead) is needed to decide whether it’s just S → s or part of a sequence in L.

d:
• The terminal d uniquely signals the alternative S → d L b.
• When d is encountered, the parser knows to match d, call the function for L, and then expect a b.

b:
• b appears only at the end of the production S → d L b.
• Its occurrence confirms the end of that S production.

The only ambiguous case is s, since it appears in both S → s and L → s L. The parser must use lookahead to determine whether s is an independent S or part of L.
